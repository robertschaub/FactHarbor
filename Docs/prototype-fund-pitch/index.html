Hello, I’m Robert Schaub. I spent 38 years in software engineering and project leadership—30 years at Phonak/Sonova. I founded FactHarbor Verein, a Swiss non-profit, and built FactHarbor’s working Alpha and Our AI Charter Runtime, a proof of concept.

My motivation is societal: facts are a foundation of democracy. I want AI to strengthen, not weaken, responsibility for decisions. Evidence is essential, but a consequential answer also needs valid authority, a decision before effect, and a record affected people can inspect and challenge.

FactHarbor has already analysed a public claim that a heat pump can be recommended for an existing residential building in Winterthur without first checking the building. The Alpha judged that claim false and showed supporting and opposing evidence, sources, limitations and uncertainty.

Evidence-Gated Agents will test the next step. For the prototype, FactHarbor will prepare assessed claims. An AI may draft an exact answer from them, but a gateway outside the model decides whether current authority and declared evidence support its release. If it stops, a different answer cannot inherit approval: it must be prepared and checked as a new proposal. If released, the recipient receives an inspectable receipt linking the answer, authority, evidence, decision and outcome.

Beyond the prototype, I want to develop evidence-gated decision-making applications and services for companies, public bodies, newsrooms and civil society. With permission, they could weigh an organisation’s policies, contracts and records alongside public evidence. People affected by an answer should be able to understand it, challenge it, and seek correction or independent review.

I built both foundations and can integrate them directly. The Prototype Fund’s coaches and cohort can challenge my assumptions, improve the recipient experience, and help me reach practitioners who can test whether the receipt is understandable and where AI adds justified value over simpler methods.

My vision is a free and fair society in which technology strengthens democracy because consequential decisions remain authorised, evidence-grounded and open to challenge.
